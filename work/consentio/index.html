ConSentío is a mobile app for everyday cooking. It brings three things together in one place: creating and saving your own recipes, organising your meals through the week, and seeing what other people actually cook. It is designed both for people who cook every day and for those who only do it now and then, so that anyone can find inspiration, plan ahead and share what they make.

Observe. Desk research showed a fast-growing market for recipe apps, where users value personalisation, planning and a strong visual experience, and a trend towards apps that connect food with physical and mental wellbeing. On social media, people asked for quick, easy recipes with basic ingredients, and liked seeing what real people cook.

Ask. I benchmarked existing apps, ran a SWOT analysis, interviewed three people of different ages and cooking habits, and sent a survey that got 60 responses. The same needs kept coming up: searching by the ingredients already at home, keeping favourite recipes organised, and a shopping list that builds itself.

“An app that tells me: you have this, so make this. Super clear, nothing weird.” — Ricky, 18, one of the interviewees.

Two users, one evening. The research was distilled into two personas: a freelance designer who improvises every night and loses time looking for ideas on Instagram, and a maintenance technician who wants short, clear recipes without odd ingredients or extra steps. Each got an empathy map, a customer journey and a needs matrix.

The same problem. Both reach the evening with loose ingredients in the fridge and no plan. Both journeys end the same way: an app that suggests something simple with what they already have, and lets them save it for next time.

From ideas to scope. I grouped the ideas into four clusters (food and recipes, mental wellbeing, diet and progress, community) and placed each one on a utility and feasibility matrix.

Must, should, could, won't. A MoSCoW pass decided what a first version needed and what could wait, keeping the app focused on the needs the research had surfaced.

Structure. The sitemap is built around five tabs: Home, Calendar, Add recipe, Shopping list and Profile. A flowchart maps the key task, from finding a recipe to saving it and organising it into a collection.

Visual system. A deep teal and a fresh lime as primary colours, red and yellow as accents, Poppins for all the type, spacing on an 8 px grid and a four-column layout on a 393 × 852 screen.

Getting started. A short onboarding sets the tone (no good or bad foods, small changes, big results) and asks for age, weight, target weight, daily calories and activity level, so the app is personalised from the first screen.

Find, cook, save. The home screen mixes recommendations, what's popular and what friends are cooking. Search works with the ingredients already in the fridge, and any recipe can be saved to the meal calendar or to a collection.

Plan and shop. The calendar shows each day's meals with their calories against a daily total, the shopping list fills itself from the recipes, and the profile gathers your own recipes and collections.

Try it. The high-fidelity prototype, built in Figma, covers the whole flow: onboarding, search, recipes, collections, calendar and shopping list.

ConSentío went through the whole design process, from research to a high-fidelity prototype: observing, synthesising, ideating and designing, with every decision traced back to what the interviews and the survey showed.
